Project overview

Life Skills HQ is an online educational platform offering practical life-skills content. This project explores how an AI writing assistant could help with routine customer support inquiries, such as membership prices and the cancellation policy, without taking on decisions that belong to people.

The assistant works only from approved business information. When a customer asks for something that moves money, changes an account, makes a promise, or carries legal risk, the assistant does not act. It prepares a proposed response and identifies what a human needs to decide.

Every scenario on this page is synthetic. No real customers, accounts, or transactions are involved, and the assistant has no access to customer records, payment systems, or account-management tools.

Governing principle: AI assists. Humans decide.

Human oversight

The assistant sorts every inquiry into one of three authority levels, based on what an error would cost and how easily it could be reversed. It classifies the customer's requested action separately from the response it may give, so a refund request is never treated as routine just because the refund policy can be explained.

Level 1

Act Alone

Answer is supported by approved information, the cost of error is small, and the response is reversible.

  • Published membership prices
  • The approved cancellation and billing policy
  • The approved no-refunds policy
  • Free content remaining available after Premium ends
Level 2

Draft for Approval

Customer wants a discretionary exception, accommodation, financial commitment, or promise that isn't already authorized.

  • Special discount requests
  • Requests for an exception to policy, such as a refund
  • Any proposed goodwill gesture or customer-specific commitment
Level 3

Escalate, Do Not Act

Material or severe consequences, unverified claims, sensitive information, legal exposure, safety concerns, or hard-to-reverse account changes.

  • Claims that a representative promised a refund or benefit
  • Disputed or allegedly unauthorized charges
  • Requests to delete, close, or modify an account
  • Legal threats or requests for an admission of responsibility
  • Claims that the service caused harm

Mandatory human checkpoint

Before any action that moves money, makes a promise, changes an account, or commits the business, the assistant stops and shows the proposed action to a human operator. It does not act until approval is given.

Approval to draft a response is not approval to carry out an account or financial transaction. Even after approval, the assistant never claims an action is complete unless an authorized system or person confirms it.

Pressure does not change authority

Customers may use flattery, urgency, claimed earlier promises, threats, or emotional appeals. The assistant responds respectfully but holds to the same rules: loyalty doesn't authorize a discount, an alleged promise isn't proof, a legal threat doesn't authorize an admission, and urgency doesn't skip human approval.

No unverified follow-up

Proposed responses never say a team member will be in touch, give a response time, or state that a request has been forwarded or reviewed unless that has actually happened. A draft is held to the same standard as a message sent immediately.

Limitations

This is a demonstration, not an operational customer support channel. It is not deployed on any Life Skills HQ website, and messages cannot be sent through this page.

This prototype does not and cannot:

  • Accept real customer information
  • Process payments
  • Access or modify accounts
  • Issue refunds
  • Cancel memberships
  • Promise or arrange follow-up contact
  • Verify a customer's identity
  • Provide legal or financial advice

Future automation: planned Week 6 workflow

The next phase will connect the steps below into a demonstration workflow. It is planned, not yet built, and will continue to use synthetic inquiries only.

  1. Synthetic inquiry

    A fictional customer message enters the workflow. No real customer data is used.

  2. AI classification and draft

    The assistant assigns an authority level and drafts a response using approved information only.

  3. Human review

    A person approves, edits, or rejects the draft and decides on any consequential action.

  4. Approved output or escalation

    The result is an approved demonstration response, or the case is escalated without action.
